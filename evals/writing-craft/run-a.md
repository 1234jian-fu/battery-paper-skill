## Title

Stack-pressure dependence of the cycling benefit of a thin-layer modification in solid-state composite cathodes

## Abstract

The cycling benefit of a cathode modification needs to be evaluated across the stack pressures at which it is used. We compared a thin-layer-modified layered-oxide composite cathode (C) with an unmodified control (U) in cells with Li–In counter electrodes at 4 and 12 MPa. After 100 cycles at 25 °C and 0.2 C, mean discharge-capacity retention relative to the first discharge was 88% for C and 72% for U at 4 MPa, compared with 90% and 87% at 12 MPa. The difference between the means thus narrowed from 16 to 3 percentage points. At 4 MPa, C also had a lower post-cycling cross-sectional pore area fraction and a lower fitted interface-related resistance than U. These observations are consistent with a contribution from improved interfacial contact, but do not distinguish contact effects from chemical changes. The modification therefore showed a larger cycling benefit at the lower tested pressure, while the origin of this pressure dependence remains unresolved.

## Introduction — final paragraph

A cycling improvement measured at a single stack pressure does not establish whether the advantage persists when pressure changes. Here, we examine this question by comparing a thin-layer-modified layered-oxide composite cathode (C) with an unmodified control (U) at 4 and 12 MPa in cells with Li–In counter electrodes. The comparisons use the same cathode active-material mass, electrode area, and sulfide-electrolyte proportion. Post-cycling morphology and impedance measurements at 4 MPa provide complementary structural and electrochemical context for the lower-pressure comparison, allowing us to assess possible links between the electrode state and capacity retention.

## Results

### The retention advantage is larger at 4 MPa than at 12 MPa

The difference in mean capacity retention between C and U depended on stack pressure (Fig. 1a). After 100 cycles at 25 °C and 0.2 C, C retained 88% of its initial discharge capacity at 4 MPa, whereas U retained 72%. At 12 MPa, the corresponding values were 90% and 87%. Each value is the mean of three independent cells, with retention calculated relative to each cell's first discharge capacity. C therefore had higher mean retention at both tested pressures, but the difference narrowed from 16 percentage points at 4 MPa to 3 percentage points at 12 MPa.

At 4 MPa, the higher retention of C was accompanied by less pore area in post-cycling cross sections and a lower fitted interface-related resistance. Analysis of four cross-sectional SEM fields from one cell per sample after 100 cycles gave pore area fractions of 3% for C and 10% for U; representative images are shown in Fig. 1b. At 50% state of charge after 100 cycles, fitting the impedance spectra with the same equivalent circuit gave interface-related resistances of 80 Ω cm² for C and 140 Ω cm² for U (Fig. 1c). These measurements associate the retention difference at 4 MPa with differences in both the morphology and the electrochemical response of the cycled electrodes.

## Discussion

The larger increase in U's mean retention between 4 and 12 MPa suggests that the modification may alleviate a limitation that matters more at lower pressure. Improved interfacial contact is one possible explanation, given the lower pore area fraction and interface-related resistance of C at 4 MPa. Neither measurement, however, separates contact effects from interfacial chemical changes. The post-cycling images also do not establish how pore area evolved during cycling. Without corresponding morphology at 12 MPa, the present evidence cannot determine whether contact differences account for the narrowing retention gap.

## Conclusion

C exceeded U in mean 100-cycle capacity retention by 16 percentage points at 4 MPa and by 3 percentage points at 12 MPa in the tested Li–In cells, identifying a pressure-dependent cycling benefit whose interfacial origin remains unresolved.

## Methods

Layered-oxide composite cathodes with (C) and without (U) a thin-layer modification were compared using the same cathode active-material mass, electrode area, and sulfide-electrolyte proportion. The cathode active-material loading was 4 mg cm⁻². Cells with Li–In counter electrodes were cycled for 100 cycles at 25 °C and 0.2 C under stack pressures of either 4 or 12 MPa, using the same voltage window. Three independent cells were tested for each sample–pressure combination. Capacity retention at cycle 100 was calculated relative to the first discharge capacity of each cell, and the reported retention values are means across the three cells. After 100 cycles at 4 MPa, cells were disassembled for cross-sectional SEM. Pore area fractions were obtained from four fields of view in one cell per sample using a common segmentation threshold. Electrochemical impedance spectra were measured at 4 MPa after 100 cycles, at 50% state of charge, over a frequency range of 1 MHz–10 mHz with a perturbation amplitude of 10 mV. The same equivalent circuit was used for both samples to obtain the area-normalized interface-related resistances.
