In the electrolyte series reported by Zhang et al., SE contained 4 m ZnOTf₂, DE contained 4 m ZnOTf₂ + 1 m ZnOAc₂, and HDE contained 2.8 m ZnOTf₂ + 0.7 m ZnOAc₂ with 30 wt% DMF relative to water, where m denotes molality. Deconvolution of the liquid-electrolyte FTIR O–H stretching bands at 3200–3600 cm⁻¹ showed a smaller strongly hydrogen-bonded contribution in DE and HDE than in SE, with the lowest contribution in HDE. This trend supports a change in the water hydrogen-bond environment, although it does not directly quantify water activity or hydrogen-evolution rates.

Anion-specific Raman fits provided complementary information on ion association. The OTf⁻ SO₃ band in SE yielded an assigned contact ion pair (CIP) fraction of approximately 36.9%, whereas the acetate band in DE gave CIP and aggregate (AGG) fractions of approximately 64.5% and 12.4%, respectively. These fractions concern different anions and spectral bands and cannot be subtracted to quantify an overall CIP increase. Spectral complexity made accurate acetate fractions difficult to obtain for HDE. Zn K-edge XANES edge shifts and changes in R-space EXAFS further supported rearrangement of the local Zn environment, although these features alone do not specify a unique coordination geometry.

Molecular dynamics (MD) simulations provided a structural interpretation of these observations. The solvent-separated ion-pair fraction decreased from 72.57% in SE to 50.41% in DE and 41.82% in HDE. Aggregation followed a different trend: the AGG fraction increased from 3.96% in SE to 33.07% in DE, then declined to 25.45% in HDE, while the CIP fraction increased from 16.52% in DE to 32.73% in HDE. HDE thus combined fewer solvent-separated pairs with more CIPs and fewer aggregates than DE. These model populations are distinct from Raman peak fractions. The reported mean DMF coordination number of 0.19 also indicates DMF participation in the simulated Zn²⁺ solvation shell. Together, the results support altered water interactions and Zn coordination as plausible contributors to improved reversibility, without uniquely establishing a mechanism of side-reaction suppression. Because salt concentrations also changed, the comparisons do not isolate the DMF contribution. The reported limitation under simultaneously very high current density and areal capacity further restricts extrapolation of this explanation.
