Zhang et al. linked improved Zn plating/stripping reversibility to changes in water hydrogen bonding and Zn²⁺ solvation across electrolyte formulations. These comprised 4 m ZnOTf₂ (SE), 4 m ZnOTf₂ + 1 m ZnOAc₂ (DE), and 2.8 m ZnOTf₂ + 0.7 m ZnOAc₂ with 30 wt% dimethylformamide (DMF; HDE), where m denotes molality and the DMF mass is referenced to water. Deconvolution of the liquid-electrolyte FTIR O–H stretching region at 3200–3600 cm⁻¹ indicated a lower contribution from strongly hydrogen-bonded water in DE and HDE than in SE, with HDE showing the lowest contribution. This trend supports redistribution of water hydrogen-bonding environments, although FTIR does not directly quantify water activity or hydrogen-evolution kinetics.

Anion-sensitive Raman spectra provided complementary evidence of altered ion association. Fitting the OTf⁻ SO₃ stretching band in SE yielded an approximately 36.9% contact ion pair (CIP) contribution at 1042.3 cm⁻¹. In DE, fitting the acetate-related bands gave CIP and aggregate (AGG) contributions of approximately 64.5% and 12.4% at 1448.3 and 1473.1 cm⁻¹, respectively. These assignment-dependent fractions describe different anions and cannot establish a numerical increase in a common CIP population. The more complex acetate-related bands in HDE indicate further changes, but their precise CIP/AGG fractions remain unresolved.

Zn K-edge XANES edge shifts and changes in R-space EXAFS features further support rearrangement of the local Zn environment in HDE, without defining a unique coordination geometry. Molecular dynamics (MD) simulations provide a structural interpretation: solvent-separated ion pairs decrease from 72.57% in SE to 50.41% in DE and 41.82% in HDE, while AGGs increase from 3.96% to 33.07% before decreasing to 25.45%. Aggregation thus varies nonmonotonically across the formulations. MD also places DMF within the HDE Zn²⁺ solvation shell, with an average coordination number of 0.19. These model-derived populations are distinct from Raman peak fractions. Collectively, the measurements and simulations support solvation reorganization as a plausible contributor to enhanced reversibility, but do not independently establish suppression of all side reactions. The authors also report that this strategy is unsuitable when very high current density and very high areal capacity are imposed simultaneously.
