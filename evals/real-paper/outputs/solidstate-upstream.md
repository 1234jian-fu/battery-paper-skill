Pan et al. (2024) reported that an elastic electrolyte comprising an AM/DMAM copolymer and an NMA–LiFSI deep eutectic mixture supported contact retention in micron-sized Si electrodes under packaging-derived pressure. Calibrated force-sensitive resistors measured built-in pressures of 546 kPa in coin cells and 52 kPa in pouch cells; these measurements establish finite pressure without external loading, but do not track pressure throughout cycling. After 20 cycles, ex situ X-ray computed tomography (XR-CT) reconstructions and cross-sections showed retained Si–electrolyte contact in the elastic system, whereas Li₆PS₅Cl electrodes exhibited pronounced cracks and voids (Fig. 5d). The elastic electrodes had cycled at 546 kPa, while the sulfide electrodes had cycled under 10 MPa. Samples were removed from their casings and sealed under Ar before imaging. The authors assigned bright streaks in the elastic sample to imaging artifacts. With terminal state of charge unspecified and no contact-area or porosity statistics, the images support qualitative contact retention within the observed regions.

Finite-element simulations offer a mechanical interpretation of this structural contrast (Fig. 5b). Models of randomly distributed Si spheres undergoing lithiation-induced expansion, assuming linear elasticity, predicted lower and more uniform von Mises stress for the elastic system than for Li₆PS₅Cl, which exhibited stress concentrations. These calculated fields support reduced mechanical disruption, but do not directly verify the proposed energy dissipation through noncovalent bond rupture. Differences in cycling pressure, formulation, preparation and counter electrode—Li for the elastic system and Li–In for the sulfide system—also prevent attribution of the experimental contrast solely to elasticity.

Electrochemical results at 25 °C provide complementary evidence across distinct cell configurations. Li/elastic-electrolyte/Si coin cells tested at 0.2 and 0.4 C showed a reported capacity retention of 90.8% after 300 cycles and an average Coulombic efficiency of 99.3%. Separately, Si/elastic-electrolyte/LFP coin full cells delivered 151.4 mAh g⁻¹ at 0.5 C, normalized to LFP mass, and showed 98.3% retention after 100 cycles; their Si electrodes underwent one lithiation/delithiation cycle before assembly. The retention reference cycles were not specified. At 52 kPa, the pouch cell delivered 11.7 mAh at 0.1 C, equivalent to 140 mAh g⁻¹ based on LFP mass. This pouch result establishes operation at lower built-in pressure, but does not establish corresponding long-term durability.
