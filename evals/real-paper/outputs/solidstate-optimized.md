Pan et al. reported contact retention in micron-sized Si electrodes using an elastic electrolyte comprising an AM/DMAM copolymer and an NMA–LiFSI deep-eutectic mixture. Calibrated thin-film sensors measured built-in pressures of 546 kPa in coin cells and 52 kPa in pouch cells; operation without external loading therefore involved finite mechanical pressure. These measurements did not track pressure continuously during cycling. After 20 cycles, ex situ X-ray computed tomography (XR-CT) of recovered electrodes showed retained Si–electrolyte contact in the elastic system cycled at 546 kPa, whereas the Li₆PS₅Cl system cycled under 10 MPa exhibited pronounced cracks and voids. The authors attributed bright streaks in the elastic sample to imaging artifacts. The pressure mismatch, unspecified terminal states of charge and absence of contact-area or porosity statistics restrict this comparison to qualitative structural evidence at the sampled states.

Finite-element calculations support a mechanical interpretation of this contrast. With randomly distributed Si spheres and linear-elastic behavior assumed during lithiation-induced expansion, the model predicted lower, more uniform von Mises stresses in the elastic electrolyte, compared with stress concentrations in Li₆PS₅Cl. This redistribution is consistent with reduced mechanical disruption and improved contact preservation. The proposed energy dissipation through rupture of noncovalent bonds remains an interpretation that XR-CT did not directly verify. Differences in electrolyte formulation, electrode preparation and counter electrode (Li versus Li–In) further prevent the experimental comparison from isolating elasticity as the sole cause of improved structural integrity.

The electrochemical results place this interpretation within specific cell configurations. At 25 °C without additional pressure, Li||Si coin cells showed a reported capacity retention of 90.8% after 300 cycles in tests using 0.2 and 0.4 C. Separately, Si||LFP coin cells delivered 151.4 mAh g⁻¹ at 0.5 C, normalized to LFP mass, and showed a reported capacity retention of 98.3% after 100 cycles. Their Si electrodes underwent one lithiation/delithiation cycle before full-cell assembly. These results are consistent with contact preservation contributing to cycling stability under the reported conditions. However, the 20-cycle images cannot establish uninterrupted contact throughout the longer cycling tests.
