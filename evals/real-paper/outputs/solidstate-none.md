Pan et al. (2024) provide evidence that an AM/DMAM-based elastic electrolyte supports contact retention in micron-sized Si electrodes under pressure supplied by cell packaging. Calibrated thin-film force sensors measured built-in pressures of 546 kPa in coin cells and 52 kPa in pouch cells; operation without external loading therefore still involved mechanical confinement. These measurements do not track pressure throughout cycling. After 20 cycles, ex situ XR-CT reconstructions and cross-sections showed retained Si–electrolyte contact in the elastic-electrolyte electrode, whereas the Li₆PS₅Cl electrode exhibited pronounced cracks and voids. The elastic sample was cycled under the coin cell’s built-in pressure, while the sulfide sample received 10 MPa. Thus, the structural contrast is notable despite higher applied pressure for the sulfide, but does not constitute a matched-pressure comparison. Bright streaks in the elastic sample were identified by the authors as imaging artifacts. Without quantitative contact-area or porosity statistics and a specified terminal state of charge, these images support qualitative contact retention at the sampled endpoint.

Finite-element simulations provide a mechanically consistent interpretation: randomly distributed Si spheres undergoing lithiation-induced expansion yielded lower, more uniform von Mises stresses in the elastic-electrolyte system than in the Li₆PS₅Cl system. However, the linear-elastic model describes calculated stress redistribution rather than measured operando stresses. The proposed dissipation through noncovalent bond rupture remains a mechanistic interpretation rather than a process directly resolved by XR-CT. Moreover, electrolyte formulation, electrode preparation and counterelectrode identity also differed between the electrochemical systems. The combined observations therefore support a contribution from mechanical compliance without isolating elasticity as the sole cause of improved cycling.

The electrochemical results are consistent with this interpretation within distinct device configurations. At 25 °C, Li/elastic-electrolyte/Si coin-cell tests using 0.2 and 0.4 C showed a reported capacity retention of 90.8% after 300 cycles. Si||LFP coin cells, assembled with Si electrodes precycled once against Li, showed 98.3% retention after 100 cycles and delivered 151.4 mAh g⁻¹ at 0.5 C on an LFP-mass basis. The pouch cell delivered 11.7 mAh at 0.1 C under 52 kPa, without equivalent long-term cycling evidence. These results establish feasibility under the reported conditions, while the 20-cycle structural endpoint cannot demonstrate continuous contact preservation over 300 cycles or justify extrapolation to larger, more heavily loaded cells.
