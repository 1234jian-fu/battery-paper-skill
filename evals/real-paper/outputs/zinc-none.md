The electrolyte series reported by Zhang, Yao and co-workers comprises SE (4 m ZnOTf₂), DE (4 m ZnOTf₂ + 1 m ZnOAc₂), and HDE (2.8 m ZnOTf₂ + 0.7 m ZnOAc₂ + 30 wt% DMF relative to water mass), where m denotes molality. FTIR deconvolution of the O–H stretching region (3200–3600 cm⁻¹) indicates a smaller relative contribution from strongly hydrogen-bonded water in DE and HDE than in SE, with HDE showing the lowest contribution. This trend supports an altered water hydrogen-bonding environment accompanying changes in Zn²⁺ solvation and DMF addition. It does not directly quantify water activity or hydrogen-evolution kinetics; changes in salt composition and concentration also preclude attributing the HDE–SE difference solely to DMF.

Raman spectra provide complementary, anion-specific evidence for ion association. For SE, fitting of the OTf⁻ SO₃ stretching region (1000–1080 cm⁻¹) gives a contact ion pair (CIP) contribution of approximately 36.9% at 1042.3 cm⁻¹. For DE, acetate-band fitting assigns 64.5% to CIPs and 12.4% to aggregates (AGGs). These fractions depend on spectral assignments and refer to different anions, so their difference cannot quantify an increase in a common CIP population. The more complex acetate bands in HDE indicate further changes in coordination but prevent reliable extraction of corresponding fractions.

Zn K-edge XANES edge shifts and R-space EXAFS changes corroborate a modified local Zn environment following DMF incorporation, without establishing a unique coordination geometry. MD supplies a structural interpretation: solvent-separated ion pairs decrease from 72.57% in SE to 50.41% in DE and 41.82% in HDE, whereas AGGs increase from 3.96% to 33.07% before declining to 25.45%. Thus, HDE does not simply maximize aggregation. The reported mean DMF coordination number of 0.19 supports DMF participation in the simulated Zn²⁺ solvation shell. These model-derived populations are distinct from Raman peak fractions and need not agree numerically. Together, the complementary measurements and simulations support solvation reorganization as a plausible contributor to improved Zn reversibility, but do not independently establish complete suppression of side reactions. Extrapolation is also constrained by the authors’ stated limitation that the strategy is unsuitable when both current density and areal capacity are very high.
